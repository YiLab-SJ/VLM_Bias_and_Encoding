Figure 1. Overview of the study design. A: Eight medical vision–language models (four CXR-specific and four generalist medical VLMs) were evaluated using chest x-rays and corresponding report impressions from MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert, and ReXGradient-160K. For each model, representations were extracted separately from the vision encoder and text encoder across layers. Linear probes were trained on each layer to quantify demographic encoding for sex, age, and race using AUROC, and to measure underdiagnosis rate for "No Finding" using false-positive rate (FPR). B: Analysis framework. Demographic encoding was assessed as the extent to which demographic attributes were recoverable from model representations, while underdiagnosis disparity was measured as the "No Finding" FPR gap between demographic groups. Layer-level encoding trajectories were related to layer-wise underdiagnosis disparity using linear mixed modeling.

Abstract

Medical vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated expert-level performance for a range of tasks, but they can exhibit demographic biases with worse performance in historically under-served groups. Furthermore, the contributions of vision vs. text encoders to such biases remain unclear. We evaluated eight state-of-the-art medical VLMs for demographic encoding and underdiagnosis bias for chest x-ray (CXR) classification on >700,000 CXRs from MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert, and ReXGradient-160K; these models included four CXR-specific models (CheXagent, CheXzero, NV-Reason-CXR, and BioViL-T) and four generalist models (MedGemma 1.5, RadFM, LLaVA-Med 1.5, and MedVersa). Across all models and datasets, underdiagnosis disparity was consistently larger in vision embeddings than in text embeddings, with age producing the largest gaps. Demographic encoding was also strongest in vision embeddings, with sex most strongly encoded. Vision encoding of age was significantly associated with underdiagnosis biases, suggesting a link between demographic encoding and downstream diagnostic biases. Although VLMs have been lauded for their generalist capabilities in medicine without relying on annotated training data, the potential for demographic encoding and downstream biases in these models could exacerbate existing health disparities and raise ethical concerns that must be addressed to ensure safe and equitable use of these models in clinical care.

Result 03

Age encoding is nonlinearly associated with underdiagnosis disparity

Figure 4. Linear mixed model analysis of age encoding and underdiagnosis disparity across vision-encoder layers. A: MIMIC-CXR. B: ReXGradient-160K. C: CheXpert. The x-axis shows age encoding AUC (ability of a linear probe to distinguish patients aged 80+ from patients aged 18–39). The y-axis shows underdiagnosis disparity, defined as the No Finding FPR gap between the same age groups. Gray points are layer-level observations from the eight VLMs; curves show fitted quadratic relationships at early, middle, and late relative layer depths. Filled points with 95% CI error bars show estimated marginal means at low, median, and high age encoding levels (10th/50th/90th percentiles). Asterisks indicate statistically significant pairwise contrasts.
